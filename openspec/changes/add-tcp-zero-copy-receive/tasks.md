- [x] 2.0 Runs on the Spark pair, with a test program in `bench/spikes/zcrx/`, before any code of group 3; results in `bench/results/`, and K2, K3, K4 and C5 of `design.md` corrected from them. Each run says what it must observe:
  - Run (milestone 31, `zccredit.c`): all six, each confirming its clause, with these differences from the text below: 4,000 frames, back to back, at each of two packet sizes, not ten thousand each on a packet of its own; the `-ENOSPC` of a call in which nothing fitted was not tried; the copy path was reached by a port without a flow rule, with areas of 512 and 16,384 pages, and showed that returned pages are free for it again only after the refill ring is flushed.
  - **The credit (K2).** Arm `IORING_OP_RECV_ZC` with `sqe->len` 256, then, after it ends, with the rest of a 1 MiB frame, on a stream of such frames each sent as one send on a packet of its own. Observe: the first receive delivers exactly 256 bytes and ends with result 0 and no `MORE`; the second delivers exactly the rest and ends the same way; the bytes of both together are the frame's (checked by content, not by count); over ten thousand frames no byte is missing, doubled or out of order. Refuted if a receive delivers more than its credit, ends with another result, or bytes are lost between two armings.
  - **A page behind two pieces (K1, C4).** In the same run: the header's 256 bytes and the rest of the first page are two completions on one page (same page, offsets 0 and 256). Return only the first; observe the page is not reused (its bytes stay the frame's) until the second is returned too. Refuted if the page's bytes change after one return.
  - **End of stream against end of credit (K2).** The sender closes inside a frame and between frames. Observe: result 0 with fewer bytes than the credit in the first case; in the second, the next header's receive ends with 0 and no bytes. Refuted if either is not told apart by the count.
  - **Arming again after an end (C5).** Cancel an armed receive part-way through its credit and arm it again for the rest. Observe: `-ECANCELED`, then delivery continues at the next byte.
  - **A full completion ring part-way through a call (K3).** A completion ring of 64 entries, a frame of 1 MiB lying whole in the socket before the receive is armed (the sender sends it and goes quiet; the receiver arms only after a second), and the program reaping completions only after the kernel has stopped posting. Observe after reaping: whether more completions come without any new packet, within ten seconds. If they do, the kernel runs the request again by itself and C5's cancel rule is removed. If they do not, send one byte from the peer and observe that the rest then comes: the stall is confirmed and the rule stays. Also observe what the first call returned when nothing at all fitted: `-ENOSPC`.
  - **The copy path with an empty free list (K4).** Make the kernel copy: steer the flow to another queue than the registered one (the flow rule removed), with an area so small (64 pages) that the pool has drawn the free list empty under a second, registered flow. Observe: the receive ends with `-ENOMEM` and nothing delivered; arm it again once a turn and count how many armings pass, and what has to happen (pieces of the copy path returned, the registered flow stopping, time) before one succeeds. Refuted for C5's bound if it succeeds as soon as any page is returned through the refill ring.
